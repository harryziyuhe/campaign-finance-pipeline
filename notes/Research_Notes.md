# Research Notes

### May 4, 2026
When looking through employee contributions to Mastercard's PAC, I noticed that a few high-ranking individuals (at the EVP level) contribute to Jon Tester (D-MT) (who sits on the Senate Committee on Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs) and Patrick McHenry (R-NC) (who chaired the House Financial Services Committee). None of the contributors seem to have direct ties to Montana or North Carolina and given the recipients' committee assignment it calls into question whether this is a strategic contribution in lieu of/in support of corporate PACs or a ideology-driven contribution as commonly assumed. This ties with the literature on executive's strategic giving and asks the question of how far down does this pattern go?

To fully examine this will be a very huge undertaking. Bonica's DIME dataset cannot match individuals across different addresses, and this is problematic as people often use their home address when making regular contributions but use their company address when donating to corporate PACs.

There are essentially two separate questions:
1. Who are the individuals that contribute to corporate PACs? Especially corporate seniors (but not board members). Are they politically active donors?
2. If they make contributions to committees/candidates outside their corporate PACs, do those contributions follow more ideological or strategic patterns?
